GuestMail handles personal data in two roles. For people with a GuestMail account and visitors to this site, GuestMail decides how the data is used and is the controller. For the guests and customers of the hotels and restaurants that use GuestMail, the venue is the controller and GuestMail only processes the data for it.

## Who is responsible at GuestMail

{{legal_name}}, tax ID (NIF) {{legal_tax_id}}, {{legal_address}}. For anything about personal data, write to {{support_email}}.

## Data GuestMail collects about account holders

- Your email address and name, and those of the people on your team.
- Your billing details. Stripe handles the payment, and GuestMail never sees or stores your card number.
- Technical records of your sessions, such as IP address and browser, to keep accounts safe.
- The emails you exchange with support.

GuestMail uses this data to provide the service you signed up for, to prevent abuse and keep the service safe, which is its legitimate interest, and to meet its legal duties, such as keeping invoices.

## Data GuestMail processes for venues

A hotel or restaurant can store this about its own guests and customers in GuestMail:

- Email address, name, language, country, birthday as day and month, a VIP mark, tags and the custom fields the venue creates.
- Stays and reservations the venue imports: dates, booking channel, room type, party size and amount.
- What happened with each email: sent, delivered, opened, clicked, bounced, marked as spam or unsubscribed.
- What a person types in a signup form or popup, and the offer code they received.

If you are a guest and want to see, correct or delete your data, or stop receiving emails, contact the venue that emails you. Every email also has an unsubscribe link that works at once. GuestMail helps the venue answer your request.

## Tracking in emails, and cookies

Emails sent with GuestMail can include a small image that tells the sender the email was opened, and links that pass through GuestMail to count clicks before reaching their destination.

GuestMail sets its own cookies when you sign in and use the dashboard: one that keeps you signed in and four that remember how you use it. It sets no advertising cookies and no analytics cookies. The [cookie policy](/cookies) lists each one.

## Providers that help run GuestMail

Some of these providers are outside the European Economic Area. Transfers to providers in the United States rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or on the standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission.

DeepSeek is in China, a country without an adequacy decision of the European Commission. What goes to DeepSeek is kept to the text of your campaigns, with email addresses and phone numbers replaced by placeholders, and your brief, without contacts or author names.

GuestMail does not sell personal data. The same list, with the notice you get before it changes, is on the [subprocessors](/subprocessors) page.

## How long GuestMail keeps data

- Account data, while the account exists.
- A deleted account can be restored for {{deletion_grace_days}} days. After that its newsletters, contacts, campaigns and images are deleted for good.
- Invoices, for as long as tax law requires.
- Addresses that must not be emailed again can be kept as a one-way hash, so that the block survives without keeping the address.

## Your rights

You can ask for access to your data, for its correction or deletion, for a copy you can take elsewhere, and you can object to a use or ask to limit it. Write to {{support_email}}. You can also complain to the Spanish data protection authority, the AEPD, at [aepd.es](https://www.aepd.es).

## Security at GuestMail

Connections to GuestMail are encrypted. Access to the servers is limited to the people who run the service. If a breach affects your data, GuestMail tells you without undue delay.

## Data processing terms for venues

When you store your guests' and customers' personal data in GuestMail, you are the controller and GuestMail is your processor. The terms that apply, as Article 28 of the GDPR asks, are the [data processing agreement](/dpa), which is part of the [terms of service](/terms).

## Changes to GuestMail's privacy policy

When this policy changes in a way that matters to you, GuestMail tells you by email before the change applies.
